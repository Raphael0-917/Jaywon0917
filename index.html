01 / RESEARCH DIRECTION

A question that connects my work

How much recovery
is enough?

Recognising a risk of failure is not the same as knowing which recovery action is sufficient. A small correction may allow a task to continue without a more resource-intensive intervention.

I hope to study how a robot can select the least costly recovery option that meets a required success threshold — and how trustworthy the predictions behind that decision really are.

PRIMARY INTEREST

Decision-making
under uncertainty

Choosing an appropriate recovery action while considering the required likelihood of success and the cost of intervention.

What should the robot do?
RELATED INTEREST

Uncertainty
quantification

Learning how to evaluate the reliability of predicted recovery probabilities before using them to guide action.

How much should it trust the prediction?
APPLICATION & VALIDATION

Robotic systems
& manipulation

Connecting software decisions to physical systems, and eventually testing their benefits and limitations on real robots.

Does it work in a real system?

IN PROGRESSFinal Year Project · 2026

Dataset-driven XYZ
trajectory correction

I am developing a learning-based approach to correct trajectories with artificially introduced XYZ position errors, using offline data without requiring a physical robot environment.

The immediate question is how to make these corrections efficiently. I see this as a foundation for studying the broader problem of selecting between recovery strategies.

Current statusData preparation, algorithm design, and evaluation are being refined. No final results are reported yet.

TEAM PROJECTMechanical Product Innovation · 2026

Drone-based infrared
solar-panel inspection

A prototype combining a drone carrier, infrared sensing, and embedded comparison logic to flag abnormal thermal behaviour against reference readings from healthy panels.

My contribution. I focused on design and hardware integration, including motors and ESCs, wiring, sensor mounting, and testing. I also contributed to sensor-data collection, processing, and detection software, adjusting the hardware in response to program behaviour and sensor readings.

Open full team report PDF ↗
System method & reported test outcome +

The inspection subsystem reads thermal data, calculates temperature features, compares them with calibration references stored in EEPROM, and sends results through Bluetooth. Its detection method is reference- and threshold-based, not a trained AI classifier.

The group report records 15 correct classifications out of 16 preliminary tests (93.75%). This small test is not evidence of general field performance or autonomous flight capability.

Source: MPI Group 5 Drone Report (Revised 6), Sections 4.3 and 7, pp. 19 and 40–41. Reported team result; not an individual performance claim.

03 / HOW I APPROACH RESEARCH

Problem first. Shared understanding.

Start with
the right question.

“What exactly are we trying to solve?”

Working across hardware and software taught me that a clear problem statement and a shared understanding should come before choosing a solution.

01

Communicate observations clearly

Share what I observe and the reasoning behind it, while listening carefully to colleagues working on other parts of the system.

02

Take responsibility for the whole connection

Understand how my work affects the team’s work, rather than treating hardware, sensing, and software as isolated tasks.

03

Revise the method, retain the question

Work on a tractable problem, acknowledge what has not yet been demonstrated, and use evidence to guide the next step.
