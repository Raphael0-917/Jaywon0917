PhD research I want to pursue

Uncertainty-aware recovery decision-making for robotic manipulation

I want to study a decision layer that goes beyond detecting failure and helps a robot choose an appropriate level of recovery.

Predict recovery successEstimate the likelihood that different recovery levels — such as continue, local recovery, or a stronger policy — can still complete the task.
Measure how much the prediction can be trustedStudy uncertainty and probability calibration so that predicted success levels correspond meaningfully to observed outcomes.
Choose the minimum sufficient interventionSelect the least costly recovery that still satisfies a required reliability level, rather than automatically using the strongest option.
Validate on real robotic systemsCompare against fixed or failure-score-based strategies and identify both the advantages and the limits of the approach.
Goal: reliable task recovery with less unnecessary intervention, tested rigorously rather than assumed.

Current FYP · In progress

Offline, Learning-Based XYZ Trajectory Correction

My current FYP uses offline trajectory data with artificially introduced XYZ position errors to study efficient learning-based correction without requiring a physical robot environment. The project remains in progress, and the data, algorithm, and evaluation methods are still being refined.

  • Offline or synthetically perturbed trajectory data
  • XYZ position-error correction
  • Designed as a foundation for a broader recovery-decision problem
  • No final results claimed yet

The project combined mechanical design, a modified 3D-printed frame, thermal sensing, embedded processing, Bluetooth output, calibration-based defect classification, and structural analysis. I focused primarily on design and hardware integration while also contributing to sensor-data processing and software-related troubleshooting.
